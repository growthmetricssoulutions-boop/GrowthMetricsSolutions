Meesho vs Flipkart vs Amazon: Where Should a New Seller Start in 2026?

Mohd Anas Khan · Founder & CEO23 November 20267 min read

In 2026, the old answer — "start on Meesho because it's the only one with zero commission" — is no longer true. Amazon and Flipkart have both cut their commission to zero on a huge share of products. So the question every new seller asks us has changed. It's not "who charges the least?" anymore. It's "where will my product actually sell, profitably?"

What changed in 2025–26

Here is the short version of what the three platforms have done over the last 18 months:

  • Meesho still charges 0% commission on every category. It earns from shipping and from sellers who pay for Meesho Ads.
  • Flipkart moved to zero commission for all products under ₹1,000 in November 2025, made its value app Shopsy fully commission-free, and in July 2026 extended zero commission to all fashion products at every price.
  • Amazon first removed referral fees on products under ₹300 in April 2025. From 16 March 2026 it expanded zero referral fees to over 12.5 crore products priced under ₹1,000 across 1,800+ categories, and cut Easy Ship fees by more than 20% for products under ₹300.
What this means: for most products under ₹1,000, commission is no longer the deciding factor. Your real costs are now shipping, returns, ads and pricing — and those differ a lot by platform.

The costs that still matter

"Zero commission" does not mean "zero cost". On every platform you still need to account for:

  1. Shipping / logistics fees — usually based on weight and distance. Heavy or bulky products eat margins fastest.
  2. Returns and RTO (return to origin) — every returned order costs you two-way shipping and sometimes the product itself. Fashion and low-priced impulse buys have the highest return rates.
  3. Ads — with thousands of sellers on zero commission, visibility is now bought through platform ads. On Meesho in particular, most serious sellers run ads to stay visible.
  4. Closing / fixed fees and GST on platform services, which vary by price band and fulfilment type.

Buyer and price-range points are general patterns we see across seller accounts, not platform-published rules. Always check the latest rate card in your own seller panel.

Our simple rule for picking your first platform

  • Unbranded, low-price product (under ₹500) — kurtis, accessories, home décor, small gadgets → start on Meesho. Fastest way to get your first orders and learn what sells.
  • Fashion at any price → Flipkart is now very attractive, with zero commission across fashion.
  • Your own brand, ₹500+ product, or something that needs trust (beauty, electronics, supplements, kitchen) → start on Amazon. Better for reviews, brand pages and repeat customers.

Whichever you pick, master one platform for 60–90 days before adding the second. Running three accounts badly is the most common reason new sellers quit.

Do this before you list a single product

Build a one-line margin sheet for each product. Here is an example for a ₹499 product (illustrative numbers):

Selling price₹499
Product cost– ₹180
Shipping & platform fees– ₹90
Returns provision (15%)– ₹45
Ads (target)– ₹60
Profit per order₹124

If the last line is negative or tiny, the product won't survive ads and returns — no matter which platform you choose. Fix the price, the packaging weight or the product before you scale.

The bottom line

Commission wars have made selling online cheaper than ever, but they've also made marketplaces more crowded. The winners in 2026 are the sellers who know their numbers: real margin after shipping, returns and ads. That's exactly what we help our clients track every month.
